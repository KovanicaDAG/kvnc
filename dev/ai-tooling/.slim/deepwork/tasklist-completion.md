### Accepted Mysticeti equivocation research (librarian, 2026-10-07)

The NDSS 2025 Mysticeti-C paper (§§II-C, III-A–III-B; Lemmas 1, 4–5) treats a slot as `(authority, round)` and permits multiple proposals for an equivocating authority. It uses DAG-causal, block-specific support; distinguishes support votes, certificates, and commit; processes slots in deterministic order; and leaves a slot undecided rather than committing later slots past insufficient evidence. Skip of an equivocated slot requires evidence covering every candidate. Safety relies on distinct-authority quorum intersection and causal persistence. Sui's `base_committer.rs` similarly examines multiple leader blocks rather than selecting first/lowest hash; tests exercise equivocation commit/skip. These papers/source are external reference, not proof that the current implementation matches them.

Inference: first-arrival and hash-min are unsafe as standalone tie-breaks when validators have partial DAG views. Before adopting a rule, the project needs a normative protocol version, explicit fault/quorum model (current code is stake-weighted), support/certificate/commit/skip definitions, safety/liveness rationale, and adversarial/permutation tests. Sources: [NDSS paper](https://www.ndss-symposium.org/wp-content/uploads/2025-929-paper.pdf), [Sui base committer](https://github.com/MystenLabs/sui/blob/main/consensus/core/src/base_committer.rs), [Sui leader-slot tests](https://github.com/MystenLabs/sui/blob/main/consensus/core/src/tests/leader_slot_decider_tests.rs).

**User decision (2026-10-07): adopt paper-faithful Mysticeti-C.** Specify its slot/support/certificate/commit/skip model and weighted mapping before implementation. Do not treat the current scheduled-leader filter as equivocation-safe. Durable decision/serialization is reconciled and validated, but the current one-hash-per-leader committer is not Mysticeti-C-safe.

Durable commit remediation has now completed. `try_commit` builds without publishing decided state; engine persists the round decision, then publishes it and delivers under a synchronous serialization lock. Regression tests cover persistence failure/retry and concurrent trigger ordering. ### Weighted adaptation review (Oracle, 2026-10-07; architecture consult, not Gate 1)

The basic weighted quorum intersection is valid: `weight(A∩B) ≥ 2Q−W > W/3 > B`, assuming fixed epoch stakes, unique authorities, checked nonzero `W`, and the selected fault bound. This alone does **not** prove Mysticeti-C's weighted indirect-anchor rule. Oracle requires lemma-by-lemma proof that conflicting indirect outcomes imply incompatible weighted evidence and that honest causal persistence carries the intersection through the anchor/link relation.

Remaining proof/spec obligations: (a) define how equivocation author blocks contribute to per-candidate support and non-support sets, with per-identity deduplication for votes, parent blocks, and certifiers; (b) prove skip and later candidate commit cannot conflict under fully validated ancestry and the same r+1 evidence window; (c) pin exact paper indirect anchor predicates, slots/order, absent proposer and empty slot behavior, liveness assumptions/backpressure; (d) persist/reconstruct immutable commit **and skip** outcomes, cursor, relevant DAG evidence, committee epoch/schedule, and signer anti-equivocation state. `V` is not a decision threshold in selected paper-faithful rules absent separate justification. No tie-break by arrival/hash. Oracle advises treating `Q` replacement as a hypothesis, not a normative safety result, until the proof is completed.

Oracle's latest proof review supplied **conditional proof sketches**, not a formal proof. Weighted Lemma 1 transfers if every block has Q-stake identity-distinct parents in the preceding round, epoch membership/stakes are fixed, honest validators create one block per round and maintain causal persistence, and ancestry validates fully; duplicate-author counts, equivocated alternative parents or mixed epochs break the argument. Weighted Lemma 3 needs both the inner Q stake of candidate-supporting r+1 authors and (for direct decision) the outer Q stake of distinct r+2 certificate authors; `CertifiedLink` itself is existential for one r+2 certificate in the complete committed-anchor ancestry. Weighted Lemma 6 is conditional on the same immutable committed anchor and complete ancestry, deterministic all-candidate enumeration, and unresolved-prefix blocking. Candidate-discovery for indirect links was separately discharged under those assumptions; this does not establish full safety or liveness.

The paper's direct-skip **prose and printed `SkippedProposer` shortcut are not equivalent under equivocation**. Oracle recommended the more conservative printed shortcut as a candidate-independent sufficient condition: direct skip only with Q stake of distinct valid r+1 authors whose fully resolved immediate-parent sets contain no block by the scheduled proposer. Empty local view or missing parent/ancestry is not evidence. This is stricter than prose's per-candidate non-support. **User approved this shortcut** and the accompanying fail-closed behavior: if multiple candidates qualify for certificate-based commit, leave the slot undecided and surface an invariant failure; no hash/arrival tie-break.
